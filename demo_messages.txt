SCAMSHIELD — 10 CHALLENGING DEMO & VIVA TEST MESSAGES
=====================================================

CATEGORY A: LEGITIMATE MESSAGES WITH ALARM/TRIGGER WORDS
(Demonstrates that the model avoids naive false positives)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Authentic Bank 2FA Authentication Code
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario: Real Chase bank two-factor authentication message.
Why it's challenging: Contains alarm keywords like "code", "expires", and "text", but explicitly states the bank will never ask for it.
Model Behavior: Low/moderate risk (45.5%). Avoids false high-risk alarm.

Raw Text to Copy:
Your Chase verification code is 849201. This code expires in 10 minutes. Chase will NEVER call or text to ask for this code.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. IT Production Server Down Alert
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario: Automated DevOps server incident notification.
Why it's challenging: Starts with "URGENT:" and discusses an emergency incident dashboard, which triggers simple keyword filters.
Model Behavior: Contextual engineering tokens keep risk at a moderate 32.6%.

Raw Text to Copy:
URGENT: Production database server cpu threshold exceeded 95% at 14:22 UTC. Engineering on-call team please review incident dashboard.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Real Credit Card Fraud Verification Check
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario: Real bank fraud department SMS inquiring about an unexpected transaction.
Why it's challenging: Contains "FreeMsg", dollar amounts, and "lock card immediately".
Model Behavior: Recognizes legitimate alert phrasing without categorizing as an outright scam (41.5% cautious).

Raw Text to Copy:
FreeMsg: Did you attempt a charge of $84.20 at Shell Gas on card ending in 4102? Reply YES if authorized, or NO to lock card immediately.

CATEGORY B: SOPHISTICATED PHISHING ATTACKS
(Disguised as legitimate corporate/legal communications)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. Corporate Single Sign-On (SSO) Credential Harvester
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario: Spear-phishing email targeting employees, formatted cleanly without spelling errors.
Why it's challenging: Uses polished IT terminology, but directs users to a raw IP address host.
Model Behavior: Detects authentication cues and structural URL risk (92.7% High Risk).

Raw Text to Copy:
Mandatory IT Security Notice: All employees are required to verify their multi-factor credentials before Friday at http://192.168.1.50/sso-login


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. Fake Legal DocuSign Agreement via URL Shortener
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario: Phishing email disguised as an NDA document review request.
Why it's challenging: Professional tone; conceals malicious payload behind a public link shortener.
Model Behavior: Catches urgency deadlines, shortener risks, and link tokens (86.1% High Risk).

Raw Text to Copy:
Please review and sign the attached 2026 Non-Disclosure Agreement. Document access expires within 24 hours at http://tinyurl.com/secure-docusign-docs
